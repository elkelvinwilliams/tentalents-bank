Founded on an old idea about what we do with what we are given.

In the parable from which we take our name, three people are entrusted with money — “talents”. Two put theirs to work and grow it. One buries his in the ground, afraid of losing it. The lesson is not about money. It is that ability, knowledge and opportunity are given to be developed — and that fear and ignorance are how they are buried.

The story

Why Ten Talents exists.

Most people are never taught how money works. The gap is filled by two industries: one that makes finance feel impossibly complicated, and one that makes it sound impossibly easy. Both profit from your not understanding.

Ten Talents was founded in 2026 by a UK-based founder building it full-time, in the open, to be the third thing: an investment hub that puts capital to work — its own, and capital provided by investors and clients — in the markets and in the projects and ventures it backs, and that teaches how money actually works along the way. Honest about risk, honest about what it is, and honest about what it is not yet. It is built for the person who wants the deeper things: Jewish principles of stewardship, spiritual intelligence, and the discipline to grow what they have been given.

We are new. We would rather grow slowly and be trusted than grow quickly and become another reason people roll their eyes at finance content.

Plainly

What we are, and what we are not.

Ten Talents is

  • A community and an education platform
  • General financial literacy — how things work
  • Honest about risk, difficulty and our own stage
  • Free to join the community; Academy membership is £15.99 a month

Ten Talents is not

  • Financial advice, or a place that tells you what to buy
  • A signals group, tips channel or “funded account” scheme
  • A get-rich-quick scheme of any kind
  • A bank, a broker or an FCA-authorised investment firm
Follow the money

How we are paid, and why we tell you.

Today, our income comes from honest places: the Academy’s paid membership, our own capital invested in markets and in ventures we back (such as Kemek Enterprise), commissions under our affiliate agreements with brokerage firms and, in time, events and programmes — things you pay for knowingly, at plain prices. Where we work with capital provided by investors or clients, it is under the applicable legal structure, regulatory permissions, eligibility requirements and written terms.

We have affiliate agreements with brokerage firms, and may be paid a commission when eligible people register or trade through an approved link. Introducing people to financial services is regulated territory, so every such relationship is disclosed on the page where you see it, and broker content goes live only once the broker has approved it.

That is the arrangement: we tell you how we are paid, and we never let a commission decide what we teach.

Where this is going
